# Actor career

Work → Special careers → Actor opens the career. Start at 18 after leaving other employment. Other → Learning → Lessons contains the single general acting-training system: three practices and one class per year from age eight. Family covers classes before fourteen; later classes cost 350 base units.

Strong released performances unlock background work, small speaking roles, regular roles, main cast and leads. Maximum skill alone does not unlock roles. Movies and TV occupy your main schedule until release; background jobs allow four signings per year and commercials two. Fame commercials remain separate.

Three agent searches and one signing are allowed each year. Candidates show clients, skill and commission. Agents find referrals, discuss career goals, negotiate commission and can be dismissed. Each offer permits two negotiation rounds for salary or profit participation; studios can counter, refuse or withdraw. Signed commissions remain binding after agent dismissal.

Project menus show script quality, genre fit, buzz and performance. Recent genre experience strengthens fit, while a narrow portfolio triggers a typecast warning. Lines, character research, marketing, stunts, recovery, cast rehearsals and on-set decisions have limited uses and interactive choices.

Release reports show role, budget, revenue, profit, salary, backend, commission, tax, net payment and critic/audience scores. High performance can earn awards or nominations. Stars in highly profitable large productions can receive three-film commitments with doubled salaries and automatic yearly productions. Read queued release notices before aging again.

Production payments occur once at release; actors receive no automatic annual salary. The existing save system preserves agents, offers, productions, credits, annual limits and pending decisions. Existing actors retain their acting lessons and fame. Only one special career can be active; competing work menus and alternate employment handlers are blocked.

## Directors, seasons and creative rights

Six recurring directors specialize in different genres. Projects identify their director and show your relationship. Working together, discussing scenes and released performances build shared history; strong relationships improve future audition fit. The actor profile keeps each director's recent memories.

Networks review TV audience response and buzz after each season. Cancellation frees your schedule while preserving earned pay. Renewal lets you leave the series or review a next-season offer at 15% higher salary. Reviewing does not sign the contract; normal acceptance and negotiation still apply.

Franchise offers now include a studio-controlled deal at double the original salary, consultation rights at 1.8 times salary, or script approval at 1.5 times salary. Negotiating rights can fail based on proven performance and agent skill; refusal leaves you uncommitted. Consultation improves production readiness. Script approval replaces character research with one choice to deepen the character or strengthen the audience hook. Signed rights carry across all contracted sequels.

Submenus have back buttons to their parent section, including Family housing → Stuff and Actor profile → Work. Clicking any main tab opens its landing page, including when that tab is already selected. Navigation preserves offers, productions, relationships and saves; decision popups retain their explicit decision/dismiss buttons.

Audition results state whether you received an offer or were rejected. Accepted offers lead to a signed production with rehearsal actions and release payment. Pending offers use the same project record after loading a save, including negotiated terms. Current-year offers stranded by older versions reappear for review. Higher-tier roles still require proven career progression.
