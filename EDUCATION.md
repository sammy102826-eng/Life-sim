# Education journey

Open **Work → Education journey** at any age. This connects the existing classrooms and college systems to new early learning, transcripts, opportunities, and funding rules.

## School calendar

| Age | Stage | Assessment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–2 | Early childhood | Informal play and relationships; no grades |
| 3–4 | Preschool | Play-based developmental reports; no GPA |
| 5 | Kindergarten | Developmental feedback |
| 6–7 | Grades 1–2 | Developmental feedback |
| 8–10 | Grades 3–5 | Subject letter grades; no cumulative GPA |
| 11–13 | Grades 6–8 | Subject marks, letters, and middle-school GPA |
| 14–17 | Grades 9–12 | Subject marks, high-school GPA, activities and college preparation |
| 18+ | College, training, or adult education | Credit-weighted GPA, credits, requirements and degrees |

The calendar is a simplified game model shared across countries. One Age Up represents one year. Grades use the familiar A/B/C/D/F scale; GPA begins in Grade 6, using a 0–4 scale with intermediate grade points. Newly started high-school GPA is calculated separately from middle school. Existing recorded report cards are preserved.

## Preschool and elementary school

Preschool has a teacher, a play group, five learning areas, sharing, stories, numbers, creative play and movement. Activities are limited per year. Learning carries a modest readiness benefit into kindergarten. Preschool reports are archived on Age Up.

Elementary retains its classroom attention, teacher, classmates, friendships and subject-practice activities. Developmental and letter-grade report cards are added to the transcript each year. Use **Open classroom** to explore learning and social activities.

## Middle and high school

The existing classrooms include teachers, classmates, attendance, subject studying, homework, projects, exam preparation, tests, clubs, sports, training, team positions, school social activities and report cards. Explore subject pages and complete their core work during each year.

**School-year decision** adds age-appropriate choices about school fairs, teamwork, homework, dances, prom, applications, research and campus pressures. One decision is available per age, with consequences for learning, wellbeing, relationships or application preparation. Complete an open decision before aging up.

High-school college preparation includes research, essays, recommendations, entrance-test preparation, applications, admissions decisions, scholarships, aid, major/minor choices and offer selection. Applications open at 17. Offers are not guarantees of enrollment: the diploma requirements still apply.

To earn a high-school diploma, pass **every final-year core subject at 60% or higher**. If you do not qualify, the adult education pathway offers three preparation sessions for an equivalency qualification. College enrollment requires age 18+ and a high-school diploma or equivalency. Professional/graduate programs keep their additional degree and entrance-test requirements.

## College

Compare program information, select a valid major and optional different minor, submit an application and review its decision. Requirements determine eligibility; admissions remain chance-based. Review scholarships and financial aid before starting. Only one program can be active at a time.

Existing college menus include university/community/trade and higher-degree programs, classes and schedules, professors, classmates, majors/minors, general education, study groups, office hours, tutoring, projects, parties, clubs, sports, coaches, teams, fraternities/sororities, and degree requirements.

New **Study → Classes & coursework** makes each scheduled class interactive: attend, study, submit a project and take its exam. These tasks improve that class’s final grade. Broad study and support activities remain available. Coursework is limited to once per task/class/year.

**Finish current semester** records the current term. Semester 1 opens Semester 2 without changing age; Semester 2 finalizes annual GPA, credits and graduation checks. Age Up resolves any remaining terms using the current preparation. A completed year cannot be repeated to gain extra credits at the same age. Use **Semester report cards** to review term marks; annual transcripts retain the full year. Failed classes earn no credits. GPA includes all attempted credits, weighted by course credits. Low GPA produces academic probation and additional study may be required to meet graduation thresholds. Graduation requires the program’s years, GPA and all credit categories. An archived course transcript remains attached to the completed degree.

## Funding

College costs are now actual bills rather than estimates alone. Each completed academic year is charged once:

1. Scholarships, grants and family contributions reduce the bill.
2. Planned financial-aid loans are recorded as debt.
3. Available cash pays the remaining cost.
4. With the default shortfall-loan policy, any unpaid balance becomes additional student loan principal.

**College → Finances** shows the policy, balance and annual payment history. Choose **Require cash for shortfall** to prevent completion of an unfunded year. Loans never create free spending money. Student debt now accrues 5% annual interest, including enrollment. Required payments are deferred while enrolled and for one additional year afterward. Choose standard (targets ten repayment years), income-based (10% of income above the displayed country-adjusted threshold), or accelerated (150% of standard) repayment in College Finances or Other → Money → Loans. Required payments are capped at 50% of income. Extra payments are capped by available cash and outstanding debt. This remains a simplified annual game model, rather than a real lending product.

## Opportunities and support

**Education journey → Opportunities & student support** offers academic advising, wellbeing counseling and learning-support plans from middle school onward.

Four placements add paid experience: community service, business, technology and faculty research. Listings show age, GPA and reference requirements. Applications can be declined. Only one placement can be active at a time; complete its project to receive the stipend and field experience once. Completed placements appear in the experience record.

Additional academic, community and athletic scholarships reduce college tuition; they do not deposit cash. Awards have GPA and activity requirements, cannot be claimed repeatedly for the same program and are capped by uncovered tuition.

Campus friends now use the same relationship schema as other friends. Greek life explicitly includes fictional fraternities and sororities, with optional community-values rush questions and subsequent activities.

## Transcript and saving

**Transcript & report cards** collects developmental reports, elementary letters, middle/high-school grades, active college reports and completed degrees. Completed college degrees expand to show course history. New education progress, decisions, placement records, aid and bills are stored in normal save slots and JSON backups.

## Semester planning and workload

College has two semesters within each academic year. The annual schedule is split into two groups of classes. Only current-semester coursework can be completed; once a term is graded, that year’s schedule is locked. The first graded term charges the annual college bill; the second does not charge it again. Annual credits are finalized after both terms. Completed degrees retain both annual and semester history.

Each term starts with 48 study blocks and 100 energy. A full-time job reserves 16 blocks; each part-time job reserves 6 (up to 12). Coursework, broad studying, faculty/classmate interactions, parties, Greek activities, team/coach interactions, sports training and internship completion share the remaining budget. Up to two restorative breaks recover energy while consuming time. Unspent blocks do not carry forward. This is a simplified planning resource, not a real weekly timetable.

## Transfers

Use **College → Transfer college / evaluate credits**. Eligible community-college or university students need GPA 2.0+ and at least 12 earned general-education credits. Applications to listed universities are chance-based and do not end the current enrollment unless you accept an offer. Each target allows one attempt per age.

An accepted transfer carries up to 30 GE and 30 same-major credits (60 total). Major/minor choices remain the same; no source GPA is imported. At least two years of target residency remain, and the target’s credit/GPA requirements still apply. The old transcript is archived in the save. Existing debt stays payable, and billed tuition is not refunded. Transfers are blocked once a term of the current year has been graded.

## Renewable aid

Scholarships are reviewed at the start of each new academic year against the most recent semester’s GPA (3.0+). Recruited athletic awards also require an active sports team at performance 60+. A missed threshold suspends scholarship funding for the next year; qualifying results can restore it at the following annual review. The Finances screen shows the review outcome. Grants and family aid currently retain their existing rules.
